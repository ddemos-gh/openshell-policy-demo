# Multi-Agent Summary

## Executive Summary

Buffalos’ natural habitat depends on which animal is meant: true buffalo include African buffalo and wild water buffalo, while American “buffalo” are bison. African buffalo are native to sub-Saharan Africa’s savannas, woodlands, floodplains, and forest edges, while wild water buffalo are native to wetland-rich parts of South and Southeast Asia. Across both true buffalo groups, reliable water, grass or aquatic vegetation, and access to cover are central habitat requirements.

## Strongest Findings

- African buffalo naturally occupy sub-Saharan African habitats including savannas, open woodlands, floodplains, river margins, and forest edges.

- Wild water buffalo are naturally tied to South and Southeast Asian wetlands such as marshes, swamps, river valleys, grasslands, floodplain forests, and seasonally flooded areas.

- Water access is the clearest shared habitat requirement: buffalo commonly live near rivers, marshes, floodplains, swamps, or seasonal water sources.

- African buffalo have a broader and more continuous natural habitat range than wild water buffalo, whose wild populations are now fragmented and reduced.

- The term “buffalo” creates confusion because American bison are often called buffalo, but their natural habitat is North American prairie, grassland, and steppe rather than true buffalo habitat.

- Buffalo behavior can visibly shape habitat through rotating grazing, muddy wallows, trails, trampling near water, and seasonal movement between grazing, shade, water, and resting areas.

## Disagreements Or Tensions

- The main scope issue is whether to discuss only true buffalo or also include American bison as a terminology contrast.

- Present-day habitat may not fully represent original natural range, especially for wild water buffalo because of domestication, interbreeding, and human-driven habitat loss.

- Circular habitat features such as wallows, grazing rings, and trails may be caused by buffalo, but they can also reflect shared use by other large herbivores or livestock.
