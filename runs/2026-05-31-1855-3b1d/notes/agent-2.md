# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

Ancestry helps clarify which “buffalo” is being discussed and why different buffalo lineages are tied to different natural habitats.

## Findings

- True buffalo belong to the bovine tribe Bovini, but “buffalo” commonly refers to several different animals, including African buffalo, Asian water buffalo, and the American bison.
- African buffalo ancestry is rooted in wild bovids adapted to sub-Saharan Africa, especially savannas, woodlands, floodplains, and forest edges where grass, water, and cover are available.
- Wild water buffalo evolved in South and Southeast Asian wetland landscapes, making marshes, river valleys, grass swamps, and floodplain forests central to their natural habitat.
- American “buffalo” are actually bison, closer to Eurasian bison than to true buffalo; their ancestry is linked to open grasslands, steppe, and prairie environments.
- Ancestry shows that habitat is not one-size-fits-all: buffalo-like bovines independently became associated with grass-rich, water-accessible landscapes, but in different continents and ecological settings.
