# Agent 3 Note

## Angle

How circular patterns shape buffalo habitat use, from grazing ranges to wallows and herd movement.

## Findings

- Buffalo often use habitats in rotating patterns, grazing an area and then moving on as forage is depleted or disturbed.
- Wallowing sites can become circular depressions in grassland, wetland edges, or muddy floodplains, marking repeated buffalo use.
- Herds may form defensive circles or tight groupings when threatened, especially around calves.
- Seasonal movement can create broad loop-like routes between water, shade, grazing areas, and safer resting grounds.
- Circular grazing pressure around water sources can visibly alter vegetation, with heavier trampling and shorter grass closest to the center.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Compare satellite or aerial imagery of buffalo-used grasslands and wetlands for circular wallows, trails, and vegetation patterns.
- Check field studies on African buffalo, water buffalo, and American bison movement ecology to distinguish species-specific habitat patterns.

## Open Questions

- How much do circular habitat features come from buffalo behavior versus shared use by other large herbivores?
- Do circular movement patterns change more with predators, water availability, or human land use?
