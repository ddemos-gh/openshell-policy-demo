# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

How to allocate only five days across Japan’s classic first-trip triangle without losing too much time to transit.

## Findings

- For most first-timers, five days is too short for a balanced Tokyo + Kyoto + beyond trip; the best split is either Tokyo-only with one day trip, or a tight Tokyo/Kyoto sampler.
- If including Kyoto, a practical split is 3 nights Tokyo and 2 nights Kyoto, using the Tokaido Shinkansen; JR Central notes Tokyo to Kyoto takes about 130 minutes by Shinkansen.
- Tokyo deserves at least two full sightseeing days for first-timer neighborhoods like Shibuya/Harajuku/Shinjuku, Asakusa/Ueno, Ginza/Tsukiji, or Akihabara.
- Kyoto needs at least one full day, ideally two, to avoid reducing it to only Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, Gion, and Arashiyama.
- “Beyond” should usually be a day trip, not a third hotel base: Hakone or Kamakura from Tokyo, or Nara from Kyoto. Adding Osaka is easiest as an evening or half-day from Kyoto, but it competes with limited Kyoto time.

- Compare official tourism sample itineraries from JNTO, Go Tokyo, Kyoto City, and Nara/Kanagawa tourism sites for realistic daily pacing.
