# Agent 3 Note

## Angle

Plan meals around Japan’s strongest first-trip food experiences, balancing one splurge with everyday regional eating.

## Findings

- Prioritize variety over prestige: a 5-day first trip should include sushi or omakase, ramen, izakaya/yakitori, tempura or tonkatsu, and one regional/street-food meal such as Osaka okonomiyaki or takoyaki.
- Book only one high-effort meal. Sushi omakase, kaiseki, and popular Michelin/Bib Gourmand spots can require advance reservations, strict timing, and cancellation rules.
- Casual food is a major part of the trip, not filler: ramen shops, depachika food halls, convenience-store breakfasts, station bento, standing sushi, and izakaya are efficient and memorable.
- Match food to cities: Tokyo is strongest for sushi, ramen, cocktail bars, and breadth; Kyoto for kaiseki, tofu, wagashi, and tea; Osaka for street food, okonomiyaki, takoyaki, kushikatsu, and casual nightlife.
- Leave meal slots open. Japan rewards spontaneous eating, especially near stations, markets, department-store basements, and busy neighborhood dining streets.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Check current Michelin/Bib Gourmand listings and reservation platforms such as OMAKASE, TableCheck, Pocket Concierge, or restaurant websites for the exact travel dates.
- Pull city-specific food guides from JNTO, Michelin Guide, and recent traveler reports for Tokyo/Kyoto/Osaka depending on the final itinerary.

## Open Questions

- Will the trip include Tokyo only, or Tokyo plus Kyoto/Osaka?
- Is the traveler comfortable with raw fish, counter dining, and higher-cost set menus, or should the plan lean casual and flexible?
