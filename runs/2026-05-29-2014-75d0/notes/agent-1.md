# Agent 1 Note

## Angle

Evaluate "best soup" through the lens of a home-style mom's recipe: comfort, repeatability, nourishment, and family memory.

## Findings

- A strong mom-style soup usually wins on familiarity rather than novelty: chicken noodle, vegetable beef, lentil, tomato, and potato soups are common contenders because they are easy to personalize.
- The best version often depends on a signature household detail, such as homemade stock, egg noodles, slow-cooked onions, a parmesan rind, fresh herbs, or a squeeze of lemon at the end.
- Practicality matters: a soup that uses pantry staples, tolerates substitutions, reheats well, and feeds several people is more likely to become the family standard.
- Texture balance is key: tender vegetables, a rich broth, and one satisfying anchor like noodles, rice, beans, dumplings, or shredded meat make the soup feel complete.
- Emotional context may outweigh objective taste; a "best soup" may be the one associated with being cared for when sick, cold, busy, or homesick.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Collect 3-5 actual family soup recipes and compare common ingredients, prep time, and what makes each recipe memorable.
- Check trusted home-cooking sources or community recipe archives for the most-saved comfort soup recipes and recurring techniques.

## Open Questions

- Is the goal to name one universal best soup, or the best soup for a specific family memory?
- Should "mom's recipe" prioritize nostalgia, nutrition, low cost, or flavor complexity?
