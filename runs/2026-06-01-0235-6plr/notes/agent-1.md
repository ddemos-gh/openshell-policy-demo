# Agent 1 Note

## Angle

The flavor case for pineapple on pizza depends on whether sweet acidity can balance salty, fatty, savory toppings.

## Findings

- Pineapple works best when paired with salty or cured meats because sweetness and acidity cut through fat while amplifying saltiness.
- Ham, bacon, prosciutto, and pepperoni provide umami and salt, giving pineapple a savory anchor rather than letting it read as dessert-like.
- The strongest version is not just “sweet plus savory”; it is sweet, salty, acidic, fatty, and browned cheese working together.
- Pineapple can fail when used too heavily, added with bland toppings, or left too wet, because excess juice can make the pizza soggy and dominate the flavor.
- Similar pairings already exist across cuisines: pork with fruit, barbecue with sweet sauces, and cheese with jams or chutneys all rely on the same contrast principle.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Compare tasting notes or sensory studies on sweet-salty and acid-fat balance in foods.
- Review chef or pizzaiolo guidance on using fruit toppings without making pizza watery.

## Open Questions

- Is pineapple better with ham specifically, or with saltier, smokier meats like bacon or pepperoni?
- Does fresh pineapple perform better than canned pineapple once moisture and sweetness are controlled?
