# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

The origin and global spread of Hawaiian pizza as a Canadian restaurant experiment that became an international menu staple.

## Findings

- Hawaiian pizza is generally credited to Sam Panopoulos, a Greek-born Canadian restaurateur, who added canned pineapple and ham to pizza at the Satellite Restaurant in Chatham, Ontario, in 1962.
- The name “Hawaiian” appears to have come from the brand or source association of the canned pineapple, not from Hawaii itself.
- Its creation fit a mid-20th-century North American pattern of experimenting with pizza beyond Italian traditions, especially with sweet-savory and convenience-food ingredients.
- The pizza spread first through Canadian and North American pizzerias, then internationally as pizza chains and local restaurants adopted it as a recognizable specialty topping combination.
- Its global spread was helped by controversy: the pineapple-on-pizza debate made Hawaiian pizza memorable, repeatable, and easy to market.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Contemporary or archival menu evidence from the Satellite Restaurant or early Ontario pizzerias showing when “Hawaiian” first appeared.
- Interviews or obituaries from major outlets, especially BBC, Washington Post, or Canadian archives, confirming Panopoulos’s account and timeline.

## Open Questions

- Were there earlier pineapple-and-ham pizza variants in Australia, the U.S., or elsewhere before Panopoulos popularized the combination?
- How quickly did major pizza chains add Hawaiian pizza to menus in different countries?
