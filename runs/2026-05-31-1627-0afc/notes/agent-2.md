# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

Trace Hawaiian pizza from its Canadian diner origin to its global spread through restaurants, chains, frozen food, and controversy.

## Findings

- Hawaiian pizza is widely credited to Sam Panopoulos, a Greek-born Canadian restaurateur, who added canned pineapple to pizza at the Satellite Restaurant in Chatham, Ontario, around 1962.
- The name “Hawaiian” appears to have come from the brand or association of the canned pineapple used, not from Hawaii itself.
- Its creation fit a mid-20th-century North American food trend: mixing sweet and savory ingredients, using canned tropical fruit, and experimenting with “exotic” flavors in casual dining.
- The style spread first through Canadian and U.S. pizzerias, then internationally as pizza chains, frozen pizzas, and standardized menus made pineapple-and-ham toppings easy to replicate.
- Its worldwide spread was helped by controversy: the topping became a recognizable cultural argument, which kept Hawaiian pizza visible far beyond its original regional context.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Check contemporary interviews or obituaries of Sam Panopoulos for the most precise account of the first Hawaiian pizza and the naming story.
- Look for early menus, trade publications, or pizza-chain records showing when Hawaiian pizza entered broader commercial circulation.

## Open Questions

- How quickly did Hawaiian pizza spread outside Canada after its early 1960s origin?
- Which major pizza chain or frozen-pizza producer did the most to normalize it globally?
