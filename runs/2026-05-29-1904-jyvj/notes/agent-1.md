# Agent 1 Note

## Angle

The flavor case for pineapple on pizza depends on whether its sweetness, acidity, and juiciness balance salty, fatty, and smoky toppings.

## Findings

- Sweet-and-savory pairings are a common culinary pattern: fruit often works with cured pork, cheese, and roasted or baked flavors because sugar softens salt and fat.
- Pineapple brings both sweetness and acidity, which can cut through rich mozzarella and fatty ham instead of simply making the pizza taste sugary.
- Ham or bacon gives pineapple a stronger case than pineapple alone because smoke, salt, and umami create contrast and keep the topping combination grounded.
- Texture is a real tradeoff: pineapple can work when drained and lightly caramelized, but excess juice can make the crust or cheese feel wet.
- The pairing is most convincing on pizzas with restrained sauce and balanced cheese; too much sweet tomato sauce plus pineapple can push the whole slice too sweet.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Compare chef or food-science sources on why fruit pairs well with pork, cheese, and smoky flavors.
- Taste-test or review recipes that use fresh, canned, drained, or roasted pineapple to see how preparation changes balance and texture.
