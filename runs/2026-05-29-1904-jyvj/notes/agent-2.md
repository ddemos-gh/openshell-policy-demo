# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

Trace Hawaiian pizza from its Canadian restaurant origin to its global spread as a mainstream and polarizing pizza style.

## Findings

- Hawaiian pizza was created in 1962 by Sam Panopoulos, a Greek-born restaurateur, at the Satellite Restaurant in Chatham, Ontario, Canada.
- The pizza combined ham or bacon with canned pineapple, reflecting mid-century North American interest in sweet-and-savory “tiki” and Polynesian-inspired foods.
- The name “Hawaiian” likely came from the brand or association of canned pineapple rather than from Hawaii itself.
- It spread first through Canadian and U.S. pizzerias as a novelty topping combination, then became normalized through chain restaurants, frozen pizzas, and international menus.
- Its worldwide spread made it both popular and controversial: in many countries it is a standard pizza option, while also serving as a shorthand debate over culinary boundaries.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Check archived interviews or obituaries for Sam Panopoulos to confirm the 1962 origin details and the naming story.
- Compare early pizza-chain menus or frozen pizza catalogs to identify when Hawaiian pizza became common outside Canada.

## Open Questions

- How quickly did Hawaiian pizza move from a local Canadian invention to U.S. and European menus?
- Was its global spread driven more by independent pizzerias, frozen food brands, or multinational pizza chains?
