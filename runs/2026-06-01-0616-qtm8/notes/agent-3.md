# Agent 3 Note

## Angle

Prioritize foods and dining formats that give a first-time visitor high variety, strong sense of place, and manageable planning effort over five days.

## Findings

- Plan one sushi counter meal, ideally lunch or a midrange omakase, because the counter experience is as important as the fish; Tokyo remains especially strong for seafood and sushi. Source: [JNTO](https://www.japan.travel/en/things-to-do/eat-and-drink)

- Ramen should be treated as a category, not one meal: try at least one shio/shoyu bowl and one richer style like miso or tonkotsu; Tokyo’s 2026 Michelin Bib Gourmand list includes ramen among its accessible specialties. Source: [Michelin Tokyo 2026](https://www.michelin.com/en/publications/products-and-services/the-michelin-guide-tokyo-2026)

- Add one izakaya or yakitori night for casual shared plates, drinks, and neighborhood atmosphere; this is often a better “Japan dining experience” than another formal reservation.

- Reserve one seasonal meal if budget allows: kaiseki, tempura, or a ryotei-style dinner. It gives structure, service, and seasonality that casual meals will not.

- Leave space for low-friction food experiences: depachika department-store food halls, convenience-store breakfast/onigiri, matcha or wagashi, street snacks, and train-station ekiben can fill gaps without overbooking.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Check current restaurant reservation windows and English-friendly booking options for Tokyo/Kyoto candidates, especially sushi, kaiseki, and popular ramen shops.

- Cross-check neighborhood food clusters against the final itinerary: Tsukiji/Ginza for sushi, Shinjuku or Shibuya for ramen/izakaya, Asakusa for older Tokyo snacks, Kyoto for kaiseki and tea sweets.
