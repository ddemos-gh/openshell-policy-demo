# Agent 1 Note

## Angle

Compare Japan’s seasons by comfort, weather risk, and crowd pressure for a first 5-day trip.

## Findings

- Spring is highly attractive for mild weather and cherry blossoms, but late March to early April can be one of the busiest and most expensive windows, especially in Tokyo/Kyoto. JNTO also flags Golden Week in late April to early May as a major domestic travel peak.
- Autumn is probably the best default season for a first short trip: cooler, drier-feeling sightseeing weather and foliage, with crowds at famous leaf spots but usually less compressed than peak sakura timing.
- Summer is the hardest season for a first-timer itinerary: June to mid-July often brings rainy season conditions across much of Honshu, while July-August is hot, humid, and busier around school holidays and Obon.
- Winter can work well for lower sightseeing crowds in cities after New Year, clearer air, illuminations, and snow destinations, but short daylight and cold weather make it less forgiving for a first 5-day Tokyo/Kyoto-style visit.
- Regional variation matters: Hokkaido, Tokyo/Kansai, and Okinawa have meaningfully different weather patterns, so “best month” depends on whether the trip is urban sightseeing, skiing, hiking, beaches, or festivals.
