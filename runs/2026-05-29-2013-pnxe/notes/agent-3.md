# Agent 3 Note

## Angle

Identify Japan food experiences that are distinctive, accessible on a first 5-day trip, and worth reserving or routing around.

## Findings

- Prioritize one high-end sushi or omakase meal only if the traveler cares about sushi enough to book ahead; otherwise, excellent conveyor-belt sushi, standing sushi bars, and department-store food halls offer better value and flexibility.
- Plan around regional staples by city: Tokyo for sushi, ramen, izakaya, depachika food halls, and specialty cafes; Kyoto for kaiseki, tofu, matcha sweets, and traditional tea houses; Osaka for street food such as takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kushikatsu, and late-night casual dining.
- Ramen is ideal for a short trip because it is fast, affordable, and varied; pick one or two distinct styles rather than chasing famous shops with long lines.
- Add at least one izakaya night for the social dining experience: small plates, grilled skewers, sake or beer, and a less formal look at Japanese food culture.
- Convenience stores, train-station bento, bakeries, and depachika counters should be treated as part of the food plan, not backup options, especially on travel-heavy days.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Check current reservation requirements and cancellation rules for any target sushi, kaiseki, or popular izakaya restaurants.
- Compare recent traveler reports or local food guides for queues, tourist crowding, and best neighborhoods for casual dining in Tokyo, Kyoto, and Osaka.

## Open Questions

- Is the itinerary limited to one city, or does it include Tokyo plus Kyoto/Osaka?
- Are there dietary restrictions, budget limits, or interest in alcohol-centered dining?
