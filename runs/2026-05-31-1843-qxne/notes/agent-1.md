# Agent 1 Note

## Angle

Pineapple on pizza can work when treated as a sweet-and-savory pairing, especially alongside salty, smoky, fatty ingredients like ham or bacon.

## Findings

- Sweet-and-savory combinations are a well-established flavor pattern: sweetness can balance salt, smoke, fat, and acidity rather than simply making a dish “dessert-like.”
- Pineapple contributes more than sugar: its acidity brightens rich cheese and cured meat, helping cut through heaviness.
- Ham pairs logically with pineapple because it brings salt, umami, and sometimes smoke, creating contrast instead of flavor duplication.
- The pairing is most successful when pineapple is used with restraint; too much juice or sweetness can make the pizza soggy or unbalanced.
- Texture matters: lightly charred or well-drained pineapple tends to integrate better than wet canned chunks added heavily.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Compare culinary references on classic sweet-savory pairings, such as pork with fruit, chutneys with cheese, or barbecue sauces with sugar and acid.
- Look for sensory science or food-pairing research on how sweetness, acidity, salt, fat, and umami interact in perceived flavor balance.

## Open Questions

- Is opposition to pineapple mostly about flavor balance, texture, cultural expectation, or pizza identity?
- Does fresh, grilled, or canned pineapple produce meaningfully different acceptance among tasters?
