# Agent 2 Note

## Angle

The origin and global spread of Hawaiian pizza as a Canadian restaurant experiment that became an international menu staple.

## Findings

- Hawaiian pizza is widely credited to Sam Panopoulos, a Greek-born Canadian restaurateur, at the Satellite Restaurant in Chatham, Ontario, in 1962.
- The name “Hawaiian” came from the canned pineapple brand or association, not from Hawaii as a place of invention.
- Panopoulos said the idea was experimental: he and his brothers were trying sweet-and-savory combinations at a time when pizza toppings in Canada were still relatively limited.
- Its spread likely followed the broader globalization of North American pizza chains and frozen/menu-standard pizza formats, where ham-and-pineapple became an easy, distinctive variant.
- The pizza’s notoriety helped it travel: debate over pineapple made it culturally memorable, turning a regional invention into a global reference point.

## Evidence To Gather Next

- Find an archived or primary interview with Sam Panopoulos, especially the BBC interview often quoted after his death in 2017.
- Check menus, franchise records, or newspaper ads from Canadian and international pizza chains to trace when “Hawaiian” became a standard offering.

## Open Questions

- Who first popularized Hawaiian pizza outside Canada: independent pizzerias, frozen pizza brands, or major chains?
- How early did the ham-and-pineapple combination appear in Australia, Europe, and Asia compared with North America?
